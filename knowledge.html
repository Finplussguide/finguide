Life Insurance

Financial protection designed to help protect dependants against the financial impact of the insured person's death, subject to the policy terms and conditions.

⚡ Quick Answer Life insurance is primarily about financial protection. The right type of policy depends on the purpose, cover required, time horizon, affordability and policy conditions.

🛡️ Main Purpose

To provide financial support to nominees/dependants if the insured person dies, according to the policy.

👨‍👩‍👧 Who may need it?

People whose income, financial responsibilities or liabilities are important to their family's financial life.

🔎 Major Life Insurance Concepts

Term Insurance

Primarily protection for a specified period. If a covered event occurs during the policy term, the benefit is paid according to the policy.

Endowment Plans

Insurance products that may combine protection with a savings-oriented component, subject to policy terms.

ULIP

Unit Linked Insurance Plans combine insurance with market-linked investment components. Investment value can fluctuate.

Nominee

The person nominated under a policy according to applicable rules and policy terms.

💡 Simple Example

Suppose a person has a family that depends partly on their income. If something happens to that person, the family may still have expenses such as housing, education, daily living and liabilities.

Life insurance can be considered as one part of a broader financial protection plan.

⚠️ Things to Understand Before Buying

  • Required protection amount
  • Policy term
  • Premium affordability
  • Nominee details
  • Exclusions and policy conditions
  • Claim-related documentation
  • Whether the product matches the intended financial goal
💡 Remember: Insurance and investment are not automatically the same thing. Always understand what a product is designed to do before comparing it.

Health Insurance

Learn how health insurance can help manage eligible medical expenses according to policy terms.

⚡ Quick Answer Health insurance provides financial protection against covered healthcare expenses. Coverage depends on the policy, limits, exclusions, waiting periods and other conditions.

👤 Individual Policy

Cover is provided for an individual according to the policy terms.

👨‍👩‍👧 Family Floater

A shared sum insured can cover multiple family members, subject to policy terms.

➕ Top-Up

A top-up generally operates after a specified deductible is crossed, according to its policy structure.

🔄 Super Top-Up

A super top-up generally considers eligible expenses cumulatively during the policy period, subject to terms.

⚠️ Important Terms

  • Sum insured
  • Waiting period
  • Pre-existing disease conditions
  • Room-rent limits
  • Co-payment
  • Deductible
  • Network hospitals
  • Exclusions
  • Sub-limits
💡 Don't compare health policies only by premium. Coverage conditions, exclusions, waiting periods, limits and claim-related features can be equally important.
🚗

Motor Insurance

Understand vehicle insurance, third-party cover, own-damage protection and important add-ons.

⚡ Quick Answer Motor insurance can protect against specified risks involving a vehicle. The type and extent of protection depends on the policy.

🛡️ Third-Party Insurance

Provides cover for specified third-party liabilities according to applicable law and policy terms.

🚘 Comprehensive Policy

Can combine third-party liability with own-damage protection, subject to the policy.

📊 IDV

Insured Declared Value is an important concept in motor insurance and is relevant to certain claims.

🏷️ NCB

No Claim Bonus is a benefit associated with claim-free insurance periods, subject to applicable conditions.

🔧 Zero Depreciation

An add-on that can reduce the impact of depreciation deductions on certain eligible claims, subject to terms.

🧾 RTI — Return to Invoice

Return to Invoice is an add-on designed for eligible situations where the policy may pay based on invoice-related value rather than only the standard IDV, subject to conditions.

⚠️ Add-ons are not automatically included in every motor policy. Always check the schedule, coverage and conditions.

Savings & Financial Safety

Build financial stability through emergency preparedness, disciplined saving and understanding safer savings avenues.

⚡ Quick Answer Savings can provide accessible money for emergencies, short-term needs and planned expenses. The right place for savings depends on safety, liquidity, time horizon and purpose.

🛟 Emergency Fund

A readily accessible reserve can help manage unexpected expenses or temporary income disruption.

🏛️ Government & Postal Schemes

India has various government-backed and postal savings schemes with different eligibility, tenure, interest, withdrawal and tax rules.

📌 What to Compare

  • Safety
  • Liquidity
  • Tenure
  • Interest or return structure
  • Tax treatment
  • Premature withdrawal rules
  • Purpose of the money
💡 A government or postal scheme should still be understood properly before investing. "Guaranteed" or government-backed does not mean every scheme has identical features.

Investments & SIP

Understand how time, risk, compounding and disciplined investing can influence long-term wealth creation.

⚡ Quick Answer Investing means putting money into assets with the expectation of achieving a future financial objective. Returns are not guaranteed unless specifically stated under the relevant product.

📅 SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan allows an investor to invest a chosen amount at regular intervals in a selected investment.

🔁 Compounding

Compounding means returns can themselves contribute to future growth over time.

⚖️ Risk

Different investments have different levels and types of risk. Higher potential return can involve higher uncertainty.

⏳ Time Horizon

The period available before the money is needed can influence how an investment is evaluated.

💡 SIP is a method of investing regularly. It is not itself a guaranteed-return product.

Retirement Planning

Plan for the years when employment income may reduce or stop, while expenses continue.

⚡ Quick Answer Retirement planning means estimating future expenses, accounting for inflation, building a retirement corpus and considering how that corpus may generate future income.

💸 Inflation

The cost of living can rise over time, so today's expenses may not represent future retirement expenses.

🏦 EPF

Employee Provident Fund is an important retirement savings mechanism for eligible employees under applicable rules.

📊 NPS

National Pension System is a retirement-oriented investment framework with specific rules and features.

💰 Retirement Corpus

The amount required for retirement depends on expenses, inflation, retirement age, life expectancy and other factors.

🧠 Questions to Ask

  • At what age do I expect to retire?
  • What could my future monthly expenses be?
  • How much inflation should I consider?
  • What retirement assets do I already have?
  • How will I generate income after retirement?

Child Education Planning

Connect today's savings with tomorrow's education costs.

⚡ Quick Answer Education planning involves estimating the future cost, considering inflation and deciding how much and how long you may need to save or invest.

🎓 Future Cost

Education costs can change over time, so planning should consider the future amount rather than only today's cost.

⏳ Time

A longer investment horizon can provide more time for savings and investments to potentially grow.

Loans & Credit

Understand borrowing cost, EMIs and responsible credit behaviour.

⚡ Quick Answer A loan gives access to money today in exchange for repayment according to agreed terms. The actual cost includes interest and potentially other charges.

🧮 EMI

Equated Monthly Instalment is a regular payment structure commonly used for many loans.

📊 Interest

The interest rate and method of calculation influence the total borrowing cost.

📋 Credit Behaviour

Timely repayments and responsible borrowing can be important aspects of maintaining healthy credit behaviour.

⚠️ Total Cost

Look beyond EMI and understand processing fees, charges, interest and other applicable costs.

Tax Basics

Learn the basic concepts behind taxable income, deductions, records and tax planning.

⚡ Quick Answer Tax depends on applicable laws, income, deductions, exemptions and other circumstances. Rules can change, so current official information should be checked.

💰 Taxable Income

Income considered for tax purposes after applying relevant rules.

📄 Records

Maintaining relevant financial documents can help with accurate tax reporting.

🔎 Deductions

Certain deductions may be available depending on applicable tax rules and eligibility.

📅 Tax Planning

Planning financial decisions with tax implications in mind can help avoid last-minute decisions.
